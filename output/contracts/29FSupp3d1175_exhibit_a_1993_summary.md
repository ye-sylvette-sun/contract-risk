# 2. INTRODUCTION

In order to provide for the future income security of its Employees, and in recognition of the valuable contribution made by its Employees, a pension plan is maintained for the benefit of Meriter Employees. Subsequent to the merger of Madison General Hospital and Methodist Hospital, the pension plans of both organizations were merged and a new pension plan was adopted effective as of October 1, 1987. The new plan attempts to preserve the best features of both of the prior plans. Special provisions apply under certain circumstances to Employees who were participants in the prior plans.

THE PLAN IS A LONG AND COMPLICATED DOCUMENT. IN AN EFFORT TO SIMPLIFY AND EXPLAIN THE PROVISIONS OF THE PLAN, CERTAIN DETAILS OF THE PLAN HAVE BEEN RESTATED OR OMITTED IN THIS SUMMARY. THEREFORE, IF YOU HAVE QUESTIONS ABOUT THE PLAN YOU SHOULD NOT RELY SOLELY ON THE FOLLOWING DESCRIPTION BUT SHOULD CONSULT THE PLAN DOCUMENT WHICH IS AVAILABLE AT THE PERSONNEL DEPARTMENT OF MERITER HOSPITAL OR ASK THE PLAN ADMINISTRATOR FOR FURTHER INFORMATION. YOUR RIGHTS AND BENEFITS UNDER THE PLAN ARE DETERMINED BY THE PROVISIONS OF THE PLAN AND NOT BY THE PROVISIONS OF THIS SUMMARY.

# 3. DEFINITIONS

The following terms that are used in this document are defined here for your convenience.

**3.1 Annual Earnings** means the compensation paid to you by Meriter for that portion of the plan year in which you were a participant in the Plan, shown on your United States Treasury Form W-2, subject to certain modifications described in the Plan.

**3.2 Break in Service** means a 12-consecutive month period which is used for eligibility or vesting purposes during which an Employee is credited with less than 501 Hours of Service.

**3.3 Effective Date** means October 1, 1987.

**3.4 Employer** means Meriter Health Services, Inc. and any related organizations that adopt this Plan.

**3.5 Hour of Service** means each hour for which an Employee is paid or entitled to payment. This includes regular time, overtime, vacations, holidays, illness, disability, and similar paid periods. There are limitations on the number of hours credited for such periods during which no duties are performed.

**3.6 Participant** means an Employee who has satisfied the eligibility requirements for participation in the Plan and who makes required Employee contributions to the Plan.

**3.7 Plan Year** means the calendar year from January 1 to December 31.

**3.8 Prior Defined Benefit Plan** means the Madison General Hospital Pension Plan, General Medical Laboratories, Inc. Pension Plan, and General Health Management Co., Inc. Pension Plan.

**3.9 Prior Defined Contribution Plan** means the Wisconsin Hospital Association Group Annuity Plan, Defined Contribution Plan, as adopted by Methodist Health Services, Inc., Methodist Health Foundation, Inc., Methodist Retirement Center, Inc., Methodist Hospital, and Allied Health Services, Inc.

**3.10 Prior Plan** means all Prior Defined Benefit Plans and Prior Defined Contribution Plans.

**3.11 Year of Service** means a 12-consecutive month period during which an Employee is credited with at least 1,000 Hours of Service. In determining eligibility to participate in the Plan, the initial computation period is the 12-month period beginning on the first day of employment. For vesting and benefit accrual purposes, the 12-month period is the Plan Year.

# **4. ELIGIBILITY & PARTICIPATION**

## **4.1 General Requirements**

**4.1.1 Eligibility.** If you are age 21 or older and have completed at least 1000 Hours of Service with Meriter in either the 12-consecutive month period beginning on the date you were employed, or during any Plan Year thereafter, you are eligible to participate in the Plan. If you are a leased employee or are covered by a collective bargaining agreement with Meriter which does not provide for participation in the Plan, you are not eligible to participate in the Plan.

**4.1.2 Participation.** If you were a participant in a Prior Plan and were employed on the Effective Date, you automatically became a Participant in the Plan on the Effective Date. If you were not a participant in a Prior Plan, and you met the eligibility requirements described in Section 4.1.1, you will become eligible to participate in the Plan on the next following April 1 or October 1 ("Participant Date"). For example, an Employee who met the eligibility requirements on February 26, 1993, would become eligible to participate on April 1, 1993; and an Employee who met the eligibility requirements on September 10, 1993, would become eligible to participate on October 1, 1993. If you do not elect to become a participant on the first Participation Date after you become eligible to participate, you may elect to become a Participant on any succeeding Participant Date assuming that you are still eligible to participate.

**4.1.3 Mandatory Employee Contributions.** Upon becoming eligible to participate, you must file with the Pension Committee a written election to participate and a written consent which authorizes the deduction from your paychecks of 1% of your Annual earnings ("Required Contributions") to the Plan. If you discontinue making your Required Contributions, you may not resume participation until the first Participation Date that is at least one year after the date you discontinued making your Required Contributions.

**4.1.4 Continued Participation.** You will continue to be a Participant in the Plan as long as you are employed by Meriter and continue to make your Required Contributions.

**4.2 Termination and Reemployment.** The rules regarding participation upon reemployment are complicated and you should contact the Plan Administrator for specific information regarding your situation. In general, if you were a Participant in the Plan at the time your employment terminated, you will participate in the Plan immediately upon reemployment if: (i) you had a vested benefit derived from Employer Contributions; or (ii) your period of Break in Service is less than the greater of five years or your Years of Eligibility Service before termination of employment. If you do not meet either of these conditions, or if you were not a Participant at the time your employment termination, then upon reemployment, you will be considered a new Employee for eligibility purposes.

For example, if you were a Participant prior to separation and had a vested benefit from Employer Contributions, you would become a Participant immediately upon reemployment regardless of the number of years you were separated from service, or the number of years of Eligibility Service you had earned previously. However, if you were a Participant prior to separation with four years of Eligibility Service, but did not have a vested benefit from Employer Contributions, and then incurred five or more consecutive one-year Breaks in Service, you would be considered a new Employee for eligibility purposes.

**4.3 Prior Plan Service.** Your Years of Service under a Prior Defined Benefit Plan (Madison General Plan) or a Prior Defined Contribution Plan (Methodist Plan) are counted in determining Eligibility Service under the Plan.

# **5. PENSION BENEFITS**

**5.1 Pension Benefit Calculations.** The amount of your Pension benefit will be determined by your Account Balance at the time you retire. Your Account Balance consists of your Required Contributions, Meriter's Contributions, and the earnings credited to your Account. If you participated in the Madison General Plan or Methodist Plan, you will also receive credit for the benefits earned under Those Plans as of the Effective Date.

Each year that you participate in the Plan, your Account will be credited with a Yearly Accrual equal to 6% of your Annual Earnings (1% from your Required Contributions, and 5% from Meriter's Contributions). If you have ten or more years of

Vesting Service (including the current year), the amount of the Yearly Accrual is increased to 7.5% of your Annual Earnings (1% from your Required Contributions and 6.5% from Meriter's Contributions).

Your Account will also be increased each year by an Investment Credit. The amount of the Investment Credit is computed differently on the amounts you contributed to the Plan and the amounts that Meriter contributed. The amount credited on your contributions to the Madison General Pension Plan prior to January 1, 1988 was 5% per year. After January 1, 1988, this rate and the rate credited on your contributions to the Methodist Pension Plan, were changed to the greater of 120% of the Federal Mid-term Rate (published by the Internal Revenue Service) or the Indexing Rate. The Indexing Rate is the rate of return credited on Meriter's contributions to the Plan which is determined each year by the Pension Committee. This rate will not be less than 4% per year.

**5.2 Benefits from the Madison General Pension Plan.** If you were a participant in the Madison General Pension Plan, your Account Balance will be credited with your accrued benefit as determined under the Pension Plan as of October 1, 1987. In addition, if you were a participant in the Madison General Pension Plan on September 30, 1987 and were making contributions to the Plan, your Account will be credited with a special Transition Credit. The amount of this credit is the sum of: (i) your contributions to the Plan as of October 1, 1987 plus interest at 12% per year; and (ii) the excess, if any, of three times the amount computed in (i) over the single sum equivalent of your accrued benefit under the Plan as of October 1, 1987. For example, if your contributions to the Plan as of October 1, 1987 were \$2,000 including interest, and the single sum equivalent of your accrued benefit was \$5,000, your Transition Credit would be (i) \$2,000 plus (ii) three times \$2,000 minus \$5,000 which equals \$1,000, for a total of \$3,000.

**5.3 Benefits from the Methodist Pension Plan.** If you were a participant in the Methodist Pension Plan, your Account Balance will be credited with the amount of your Account Balance under the Methodist Plan as of October 1, 1987.

**5.4 Separation from Service Prior to October 1, 1987.** If you separated from service prior to October 1, 1987 and were receiving benefits from a Prior Plan, you will continue to receive benefits in the same form and amount as you had elected under the Prior Plan. If you separated from service with a deferred vested benefit under a Prior Plan, you continue to be entitled to receive the same benefits you were entitled to receive under the Prior Plan.

**5.5 Termination and Reemployment.** The rules regarding computation of benefits upon reemployment are very complicated. You should contact the Plan Administrator for specific information on your situation. Your benefits will depend upon factors such as when you terminated employment, whether or not you had a vested benefit at the time of termination, how long you were gone, and the actions that you take upon reemployment.

# **6. BENEFITS**

**6.1 General Distribution Rules.** You may receive a distribution of benefits upon termination of employment due to normal retirement, postponed retirement, disability retirement or separation from service. Your beneficiary is entitled to receive your benefits upon your death. Benefits may also be distributed to a former spouse of a Participant pursuant to a qualified domestic relations order. A qualified domestic relations order is an order entered in a divorce proceeding which requires the Trustee to distribute benefits to a Participant's former spouse. Distributions you receive from the Plan are subject to income tax. You should discuss the tax consequences of receiving benefits with your tax advisor.

**6.1.1 Normal Retirement.** Normal Retirement Age under the Plan is age 65. You are entitled to begin to receive your benefits not later than the first day of the month after you reach age 65.

**6.1.2 Early Retirement.** Early retirement benefits are payable if you retire after reaching age 55. The amount of the benefit, however, is reduced because the benefits will be paid to you over a longer period of time. For Plan Years beginning after December 31, 1988, normal retirement benefits are reduced by 2% for each year (2/12th% per month) that you retire prior to normal retirement. Prior to January 1, 1989, the retirement benefits were reduced by 5% per year for retirement between ages 60 and 65 plus an additional 3% per year for retirement between ages 55 and 60. For example, if you retire at age 62, after December 31, 1988, and your normal retirement benefit is \$500 per month, your monthly benefit will be reduced by 6% (2% times 3) resulting in a benefit of \$500 minus 6% times \$500 equals \$470 per month.

**6.1.3 Postponed Retirement.** If you continue to work for Meriter after normal retirement age, and continue to make your Required Contributions, your account will continue to be credited with Yearly Accruals and interest accruals until you retire. Pension benefits will become payable to you when you retire. If you continue employment beyond age 70 1/2, benefits must begin to be paid to you even though you continue to be employed by Meriter.

**6.1.4 Disability Retirement.** If you become disabled while employed by Meriter, your benefits are fully vested regardless of your years of vesting service. You will be eligible to receive a disability retirement pension beginning on the first day of the month after you reach normal retirement age. The requirement that you make contributions to the Plan is waived during the period you are disabled. Yearly Accruals and interest accruals continue to be credited to your account based on your earnings at the time you became disabled.

**6.1.5 Separation From Service.** If you leave employment with Meriter, the amount of benefits you are entitled to receive will depend upon your vested interest in your account balance. Vesting refers to your ownership rights in your account balance.

You are always 100% vested in your Mandatory Contributions to the Plan and in the earnings credited on your contributions. You have the right to receive a distribution of these contributions and earnings at anytime after you separate from service.

On the amounts contributed by Meriter, you earn one year of vesting service for each Plan Year that you are credited with at least 1000 hours of service and contribute to the Plan. After you have earned 5 years of vesting service, your account balance including the amount contributed by Meriter and the earnings on the Meriter Contributions, becomes fully vested. If you terminate employment prior to being credited with 5 years of vesting service, you will have no vested interest in the Meriter Contributions or earnings on the Meriter Contributions credited to your account.

If you participated in a Prior Plan, all of your years of Participation in the Prior Plan are counted toward the vesting requirements under the new Plan. A special rule applies if you are credited with less than 5 years of vesting service and had been a participant in the prior Methodist Plan. Under these circumstances, the vesting schedule of that Plan still applies to Meriter's Contributions. However, after you are credited with a total of 5 years of vesting service under the Methodist Plan and the new Plan, you will be 100% vested in all of the contributions.

**6.2 Death Benefits.** If you die before payment of your benefits begins, your entire account balance, including your contributions and Meriter's Contributions, is paid to your surviving spouse or other designated beneficiary.

**6.2.1 Payments to Spouse.** If you were married at the time of your death, under normal circumstances your Plan benefits will be paid to your surviving spouse. If you were not married, or your spouse has consented in writing to your naming another beneficiary, payment will be made to your named beneficiary.

**6.2.2 Beneficiary Designation.** Forms are available from the Plan Administrator for you to name a beneficiary. For the beneficiary designation to be effective it must be filed with the Plan Administrator. A new beneficiary designation cancels all prior designations. The designation of a beneficiary other than your spouse must be approved by your spouse in writing on forms provided by the Plan Administrator and filed with the Plan Administrator. Your spouse's consent is irrevocable if given.

## **6.3 Method of Payment Upon Retirement.**

**6.3.1 Normal Form of Payment.** The Plan allows you to choose from a number of payment options upon retirement. If you are single when you begin to receive payments, your account balance normally will be converted to a life annuity under which you will receive equal monthly payments for the rest of your life. Upon your death, payment of benefits terminates. If you are married when you begin to receive benefits, your account balance normally will be converted into an annuity which provides for equal monthly payments to you for the rest of your life, and if you die before your spouse, your spouse will continue to receive 50% of your monthly payment for the rest of his or her life. The

monthly payment that you would otherwise receive will be reduced to allow for the continued payments to your spouse after your death.

**6.3.2 Optional Forms of Payment.** You may choose to have your account balance paid in a form other than the normal form described above. The following optional payment forms are available:

- (i) **Lump Sum.** You may elect to receive your account balance in a single lump sum payment. If you are younger than age 55 at the time you receive payment, however, you must deposit the payment (other than your Required Contributions) into an IRA or another employer's qualified retirement plan;
- (ii) **Beneficiary Annuity.** You may elect to receive monthly payments for your life and upon your death have 50%, 75%, or 100% of your benefit paid for the life of any beneficiary you name. The amount of the payment to you will depend upon the percentage of the benefit that will be paid to your named beneficiary, and the ages of you and the beneficiary;
- (iii) **Certain and Continuous Annuity.** Under this form of payment, you will receive monthly payments for your life with a guaranteed minimum number (either 60 or 120) of payments. If you die before receiving the guaranteed number of payments, your designated beneficiary will receive the remaining payments.

**6.4 Method of Payment Upon Termination of Employment.** If you terminate employment with Meriter before reaching age 55, you may choose from a number of options for payment of your benefits.

**6.4.1 Nonvested Benefits.** If your interest in Meriter's Contributions is not vested, and your contributions with interest total less than \$3,500, your account balance will be paid to you in a lump sum. If your contributions and interest total more than \$3,500, you may elect to leave the funds in the Plan where they will continue to accrue interest until distributed.

**6.4.2 Vested Benefits.** If your interest in Meriter's Contributions is vested, you may elect to leave the entire account balance in the Plan and continue to accrue interest. You also may elect to withdraw your account balance provided that you deposit the distribution (other than your Required Contributions) into an IRA or another employer's qualified retirement plan.

**6.5 Mandatory Distribution.** If your account balance derived from Meriter Contributions and your Required Contributions is \$3,500 or less, your vested benefits will be paid to you in a lump sum.

**6.6 Benefit Insurance.** Benefits under this Plan are insured by the Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation (PBGC) if the Plan terminates. Generally, the PBGC

guarantees most vested normal retirement benefits, early retirement benefits, and certain disability and survivor's pensions. However, PBGC does not guarantee all types of benefits under covered plans, and the amount of benefit protection is subject to certain limitations.

The PBGC guarantees vested benefits at the level in effect on the date of plan termination. However, if a plan has been in effect less than 5 years before it terminates, or if benefits have been increased within the 5 years before plan termination, the whole amount of the plan's vested benefits or the benefit increase may not be guaranteed. In addition, there is a ceiling on the amount of monthly benefit that PBGC guarantees, which is adjusted periodically.

For more information on the PBGC insurance protection and its limitations, ask your Plan Administrator or the PBGC. Inquiries to the PBGC should be addressed to the Office of Communications, PBGC, 2020 K Street NW, Washington, DC 20006. The PBGC Office of Communications may also be reached by calling (202) 778-8800.

# **7. CLAIMS PROCEDURE**

**7.1 General Procedure.** If you are filing a claim for benefits under the Plan, you must use the forms provided by the Plan Administrator. The Plan Administrator will notify you in writing of the disposition of your claim within 30 days after receipt of the claim. Any denial or partial denial of a claim will: (i) specify the reason or reasons for the denial; (ii) identify the Plan provisions on which denial is based; (iii) identify any additional material that may be necessary for you to perfect your claim and explain why such material is necessary; and (iv) explain the Plan's appeal procedure.

**7.2 Appeals.** You may appeal a denial of your claim under the Plan by filing a written request with the Plan Administrator within 60 days after the receipt of the Plan Administrator's denial or partial denial of your claim. The Plan Administrator must give you a full and fair review of your claim and allow you to review documents and to submit issues and comments in writing. The Plan Administrator may hire legal counsel to render legal advice in its review. Within 60 days after the receipt of your appeal, the Plan Administrator must render a decision on your appeal. The decision must include specific reasons for the decision, written in a manner which you can understand, with specific references to the Plan provisions on which the decision is based.

# **8. PLAN TERMINATION**

Meriter has the right to terminate the Plan at anytime. Upon termination, you will become 100% vested in the present value of your accrued benefit, and benefits will be distributed to you in accordance with law. The assets of the trust fund will be allocated or divided among Participants and Beneficiaries in accordance with the following priorities:

- (i) To fund benefits for Retired Participants and their beneficiaries to whom payment commenced at least 3 years prior to the date of termination and Participants who could have retired and received payment of their benefits at least 3 years prior to the date of termination.
- (ii) To all benefits guaranteed and insured by the Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation (PBGC).
- (iii) To all other vested accrued benefits not insured by the PBGC.
- (iv) To all other accrued benefits provided under the Plan.

Any excess funds will revert to Meriter.

# **9. ASSIGNMENT OF BENEFITS**

Normally, you may not transfer or assign your interest in the Plan; that is, you may not sell it, use it as collateral, or otherwise give it away. Your creditors may not attach or garnish your interest in the Plan. However, the Plan Administrator may be required to use some or all of your benefits to pay court-ordered alimony, child support, or marital property rights to a spouse, former spouse, child or other dependent. The court order must follow a certain form and contain certain information. If it does not, the Administrator will not honor it. If it does, the Administrator must comply with it. The Administrator alone will determine whether the court order must be followed.

# **10. STATEMENT OF ERISA RIGHTS**

As a Participant in the Plan you are entitled to certain rights and protections under the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (ERISA). ERISA provides that all Plan Participants shall be entitled to:

- (i) Examine, without charge, at the Plan Administrator's office and at other specified locations, such as work sites and union halls, all Plan documents, including insurance contracts, collective bargaining agreements and copies of all documents filed by the Plan with the U.S. Department of Labor, such as detailed annual reports and plan descriptions.
- (ii) Obtain copies of all Plan documents and other Plan information upon written request to the Plan Administrator. The Administrator may make a reasonable charge for the copies.

- (iii) Receive a summary of the Plan's annual financial report. The Plan Administrator is required by law to furnish each Participant with a copy of this Summary Annual Report.
- (iv) Obtain a statement telling you whether you have a right to receive a pension at normal retirement age (age 65) and, if so, what your benefits would be at normal retirement age if you stop working under the Plan now. If you do not have a right to a pension, the statement will tell you how many more years you have to work to get a right to a pension. This statement must be requested in writing and is not required to be given more than once a year. The Plan Administrator must provide the statement free of charge.

In addition to creating rights for Plan Participants, ERISA imposes duties upon the people who are responsible for the operation of the employee benefit plan. The people who operate your Plan, called "fiduciaries" of the Plan, have a duty to do so prudently and in the interest of you and other Plan Participants and beneficiaries. No one, including your Employer, your union, or any other person, may fire you or otherwise discriminate against you in any way to prevent you from obtaining a pension benefit or exercising your rights under ERISA.

If your claim for a pension benefit is denied in whole or in part, you must receive a written explanation of the reason for the denial. You have the right to have the Plan Administrator review and reconsider your claim. Under ERISA, there are steps you can take to enforce the above rights. For instance, if you request materials from the Plan Administrator and do not receive them within 30 days, you may file suit in a federal court. In such case, the court may require the Plan Administrator to provide the materials and pay you up to \$100 a day until you receive the materials, unless the materials were not sent because of reasons beyond the control of the Administrator.

If you have a claim for benefits which is denied or ignored, in whole or in part, you may file suit in a state or federal court. If it should happen that Plan fiduciaries misuse the Plan's money, or if you are discriminated against for asserting your rights, you may seek assistance from the U.S. Department of Labor, or you may file suit in a federal court. The court will decide who should pay court costs and legal fees. If you are successful the court may order the person you have sued to pay these costs and fees. If you lose, the court may order you to pay these costs and fees, for example, if it finds your claim is frivolous.

If you have any questions about your Plan, you should contact the Plan Administrator. If you have any questions about this statement or about your rights under ERISA, you should contact the nearest Area Office of the U.S. Labor-Management Services Administration, Department of Labor.
