I. Executive Summary

MORTGAGE FUND °08 LLC (the “Company”), sponsored by the principals of R.E. Loans, LLC, offers a
new and attractive opportunity for qualified investors to achieve superior returns with a conservative risk
profile through the purchase of promissory notes (the “Notes”) issued by the Company. The Company is
targeting to return approximately 8.00% or more per year on the Noteholders investments. The Notes will
be secured by the assets of the Company.

The Company will be formed as a Delaware limited liability company for the purpose of lending capital
raised by the sale of Notes to borrowers (the “Portfolio Borrowers”) to finance the ownership,
entitlement, development or rehabilitation of commercial real estate or for other real estate related
purposes throughout the United States (the “Portfolic Loans”), The Portfolio Loans will be secured by
liens against real property and associated personal property.

The Company will be managed by its manager, The Mortgage Fund LLC, a California limited liability
company formed for this purpose (the “Manager” or “The Mortgage Fund LLC’). The Manager is
owned and led by Walter Ng and Kelly Ng (the “Managing Directors” or “Principals”), who together
bring to the Company more than 81 years of experience in mortgage lending and investing among them.
The Manager expects to hire Bar-K, Inc. to provide Portfolio Loan origination, brokerage and
administration services to the Company (“Loan Services”) as “Servicer”, comparable to the services that
Bar-K, Inc. provides to R.E. Loans, LLC and that it provided to its predecessor investment funds. Bar-K
Inc, is managed by Barney Ng, and owned by Bamey and Kelly Ng. Walter Ng, Kelly Ng and Barney Ng
also manage and own, with Bruce Horwitz, M.D., B-4 Partners, LLC, the sponsor of R.E. Loans LLC,
and were sponsors of prior investment funds to R.E. Loans, LLC.

In order to participate in this opportunity, each prospective investor will subscribe to purchase Notes
pursuant to a Note Purchase Agreement with the Company by “loaning” their investment capital to the
Company (their “Loan Amounts”). Notes will be issued to investors whose subscriptions are accepted by
the Company (“Nofeholders”), in its discretion, at the next closing held by the Company, The initial
closing is expected to be on or before December 3, 2007, and the Company expects to hold closings on
the first day of each calendar month thereafter through May 1, 2009, although there is no assurance that
there will be a subsequent closing or that a closing will be held each month, The Company has not
targeted a minimum or maximum amount of capital to be raised by a sale of Notes. The Manager, the
Principals, their family members, and their affiliates may also purchase Notes on the same terms as the
Notes purchased by investors.

The Notes will bear simple interest at a rate determined quarterly by the Managing Directors, subject to a
minimum average rate of eight percent (8%) annualized, The Notes will be secured by the assets of the
Company. Regardless of when purchased, each Note will have a term ending December 31, 2014,
subject, in the discretion of the Company, to a six month extension that would be applicable to all
outstanding Notes. Interest will be payable quarterly, but payment of principal will be deferred, at the
discretion of the Company, until the maturity of the Note, although principal may be prepaid at any time
without penalty.

The Notes are an illiquid and long-term investment. No investor should subscribe to purchase a Note
unless it is prepared to hold the Note until its maturity. Although the Company may, in its discretion,
prepay a Note at any time, a Noteholder may not request prepayment of a Note until it has held the Note
for a minimum of one year from the date of its issue, and the granting of such request is within the sole
discretion of the Company. The Notes will not be transferable except under very limited circumstances
and then only in the discretion of the Manager. There is no secondary market for sale of the Notes and
none is expected to develop.

II. Private Mortgage Lending

The Company intends to invest in conservatively underwritten real estate mortgage loans with borrowers
who are experienced in real estate operations and finance by directly controlling the sourcing, funding and
administration of such loans based upon criteria the Principals have found to be successful in the past.
The Company will not operate in the sub-prime mortgage market and will not source loans for the
purpose of resale. Instead, it will focus the portfolio on loans secured by quality real estate for generation
of income to the Company.

Lending Market Opportunities

There is a substantial demand for private capital that can be funded on a timely basis. The Managers
believe this is true in a rising or falling real estate market.

Investor Opportunities

To date, the Principais and their affiliates have a proven lending record of sponsoring investments in well-
diversified performing loans that produce solid returns to their investors. To date, no investor has
experienced a loss of investment principal through investment vehicles sponsored by the Principals or
their affiliates. Although there is no assurance that investors in the Company will experience similar
results, the Manager believes that the past real estate lending success of the Principals and their affiliates
will generate comparable opportunities for the Company and its Noteholders.

RE. Loans

The Principals and their affiliates, through B-4+ Partners, are currently sponsoring and managing R.E.
Loans, LLC, a private real estate lender. As of the date of this Memorandum, R.E. Loans has a portfolio
of over 60 secured mortgage loans in diverse markets with aggregate principal capital of $750 million.
Over its lifetime, R.E. Loans has made 325 mortgage loans with an aggregate principal of $1.5 billion and
generated returns for investors averaging 8.5% per annum.

Prior Limited Partnerships

R.E, Loans was formed in 2002 as a new investment fund by a merger of nine limited partnerships
sponsored and managed by predecessors to B-4 Partners. These nine limited partnerships were
responsible for more than 500 mortgage loans with aggregate principal of approximately $150 million.
Prior to the merger, these partnerships had diverse portfolios averaging greater than 8% return to investors
per annum, on a weighted basis.

Mortgage Servicing and Administration: Bar-K, Inc.

Bar-K, Inc, the Company’s Servicer, brings to the Company 33 years of experience in the origination,
management, work-out and collection of real estate mortgage loans. To date, Bar-K, Inc. has a 100%
collection record with respect to loans it administers. This experience has contributed greatly to the
success of R.E. Loans and its nine predecessor limited partnership, none of which experienced a loss of
principal or interest with respect to their mortgage loan portfolio.

NO FIDUCIARY RESPONSIBILITY OF MANAGER TO NOTEHOLDERS

Under Delaware law, a manager is accountable to a limited liability company’s members as a fiduciary,
which means that a manager is required to exercise good faith and integrity with respect to company
affairs. This fiduciary duty is in addition to those other duties and obligations of, and limitations on, the
Manager which are set forth in the Operating Agreement. At the time of this offering, the Manager is also
the sole member of the Company. The Noteholders do not have a member’s interest in the Company, but
are solely creditors of the Company. Accordingly, the Manager does not have a direct fiduciary
obligation to the Noteholders nor, at the time of this offering, does it have a fiduciary obligation to the
Company’s members or contractual obligation under the Operating Agreement independent of its own
interests in the Company.

The Company, however, will enter into certain contractual operating covenants and commitments to the
Noteholders pursuant to the Note Purchase Agreement, the Notes and the Security Agreement, the breach
of which by the Company may give the Noteholders a cause of action against the Company, which would
be adverse to the Manager’s interest in the Company.

The Operating Agreement provides that the Manager shall have no liability to the Company for losses
resulting from errors in judgment or other acts or omissions, unless the Manager is guilty of fraud, bad
faith or willful misconduct. The Operating Agreement also provides that the Company shall indemnify
the Manager and Servicer against liability and related expenses (including reasonable attorneys’ fees and
costs) incurred in dealing with the Company, Noteholders or third parties, so long as no fraud, bad faith or
willful misconduct on the part of the Manager or Servicer is involved. A successful indemnification of
the Manager or Servicer or any litigation that may arise in connection with their indemnification could
deplete the assets of the Company, thereby reducing the assets available to pay the Notes. Therefore,
Noteholders may have a more limited opportunity of recovery then they would have absent these
provisions in the Operating Agreement.

In addition to its contracted obligations to the Noteholders under the Note Purchase Agreement, the Notes
and the Security Agreement, the Company has certain obligations under federal securities laws in
connection with the offering and sale of the Notes, the preparation of this Memorandum, and
representations to prospective investors and communications to Noteholders regarding the condition of
the Company, certain conflicts of interest, and the terms of this offering. There are also certain state and
federal laws that govern the respective rights and obligations of the Company with respect to its creditors,
and the rights and remedies of a creditor against its obligor.

Accordingly, Noteholders must rely on the good faith and integrity of the Manager to comply with the
laws, rules and regulations governing the offering of the Notes for purchase, the Company’s relationship
with the Noteholder, and to cause the Company to meet its obligations under the Note Purchase
Agreement, the Notes and the Security Agreement.

The Company’s business operations and affairs will be managed entirely by the Manager, which are
subject to certain conflicts of interest. (See “Conflicts of Interest.”)

Original Issue Discount

The Company may be deemed for tax purposes to pay to the holders of Notes “additional interest”, as
such concept is described in the following discussion, Consequently, the Company will treat the Notes as
contingent debt instruments subject to the "non-contingent bond method" of reporting interest as set forth
in applicable Treasury Regulations. As a result of the classification of the Notes as contingent debt
instruments, any gain realized on the sale or exchange of the Notes may be treated as ordinary income, in
whole or in part. See “Gain or Loss on Disposition".

Under the non-contingent bond method, the issuer of a contingent debt instrument must first determine
the Comparable Yield. The Comparable Yield is the yield at which the Company could issue a debt
instrument with terms and conditions similar to those of the contingent payment debt instrument. The
Company has selected 8% as its Comparable Yield, and this rate will be used by the Company for
purposes of determining its own taxable income and for any required information reporting. Since there
is no direct authority in this area, it is uncertain whether the Internal Revenue Service will agree with this
determination and may require a higher or lower Comparative Yield. If the Internal Revenue Service
determines that the Comparable Yield should be higher, a Noteholder will be required to accrue the
difference between the Comparable Yield selected by the Company and such higher Yield as additional
interest income earned each year, even though there are no payments of contingent interest in such year.

Under the non-contingent bond method, if the interest payable for any period is greater or less than the
amount projected, the amount of income included for that period would be either increased or decreased
accordingly. Any net increase in the projected contingent payments for a taxable year (e.g., a
determination of the amount due pursuant to the additional interest) is treated by a holder as additional
interest income.

Please note that the Comparable Yield is supplied by the Company solely for computing income under
the non-contingent bond method for federal income tax purposes, and does not constitute a projection or
representation as to the amounts that Noteholders will actually receive with respect to their Notes.

Gain or Loss on Disposition

If a Note is sold, redeemed or otherwise disposed of, the selling Noteholder will recognize gain or loss
equal to the difference between the amount realized from the disposition and the selling Noteholder's
adjusted basis in such Note. The adjusted basis generally will equal the cost of such Note to the seller,
increased by any original issue discount on such Note included in the seller's income and reduced (but not
below zero) by any non-contingent payments on the Note. Because the Notes are characterized as
contingent debt instruments under the Treasury Regulations, any gain recognized upon a sale, exchange,
retirement, or other disposition of a Note will generally be ordinary income and any loss will be ordinary
to the extent of prior ordinary income inclusion.

Tax-Exempt Entities

The income earned by qualified pension, profit-sharing and stock bonus plans, and IRA’s or other tax-
exempt entities generally is exempt from federal income taxation, except to the extent of “unrelated
business taxable income” (UBT) in excess of $1,000 in any year.

As noted above, it is intended that the interest payable with respect to the Notes will be characterized as
interest for federal income tax purposes and the Notes will not constitute an equity investment in the
Fund, with the result that amounts received with respect to the Notes will not constitute UBTI to the
Noteholders.

If the IRS were successfully to characterize the Noteholders as members in the Company, due to the
nature of the source of the Fund’s revenues as portfolio interest income, amounts received with respect to
the Notes would not be treated as UBTI to the Noteholders. Income to equity investors with respect to
Portfolio Loans funded partially by Company Borrowings would constitute “debt financed property” and
generate UBTI to the investor. Similarly, income to investors deemed partners with respect to profits to
the Company earned from resale or lease of properties acquired by the Company through foreclosure that
are encumbered by senior mortgages would also generate UBTI. However, in the past, it has been the
practice, although not the obligation, of investment funds sponsored by the Manager’s affiliates to sell
defaulting portfolio loans to the Servicer or its affiliates in order to avoid generation of UBTI in
connection with possible foreclosures. The Company may continue such practice, although it is not
obligated to do so. However, if the Company does continue the practice, it is possible that the IRS will
consider the affiliated nature of the Servicer and disregard such sale for purposes of attributing UBTI to
investors who are deemed partners,

In addition, the IRS has concluded in a General Counsel Memorandum that guaranteed payments, such as
the fixed payments provided for in the Notes, should be treated as interest for tax purposes if paid to a
member and not as income from the Company’s investments. However, a general counsel memorandum
may not be cited as authority. No assurances can be given that if the Notes are treated as equity,
"interest" payments made with respect to the Notes would constitute deductible guaranteed payment(s).
